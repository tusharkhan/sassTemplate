About us

Building the future of real-time communication

We started WaveNode because shipping live video meant becoming a media company first and a product company second. Nine years later, the infrastructure is our problem and the product is yours.

Our mission

Latency is a product decision

Every hundred milliseconds between a speaker and a listener changes what the two of them can do together. Under 50ms a conversation feels ordinary; over 400ms people start talking over each other and give up on the format entirely.

That threshold decides which products are possible. Auctions, classrooms, co-watching, live shopping, remote music — none of them survive a delay you can hear. We build the network that keeps them on the right side of it, and we publish our numbers so you can hold us to them.

We are 84 people across Berlin, Singapore and Austin. We are profitable, we have taken one round of funding, and we intend to still be here when the streams we carry are a decade old.

Our story

How we got here

  1. 2017

    A conference call that would not stop breaking

    Two of us were building a remote music-teaching tool and spent more time on jitter buffers than on lessons. We shipped the buffer code as a library instead, and people used it for everything except music.

  2. 2019

    First edge network, eleven cities

    We stopped renting other people’s delivery and started placing our own hardware. Median latency halved overnight and our margins stopped being someone else’s decision.

  3. 2021

    Captions, then the whole AI toolkit

    Customers kept asking for transcripts. Building live captions taught us that the same models could chapter, clip and summarise a stream, so we shipped all four behind one flag.

  4. 2023

    SOC 2, then the enterprise plan

    Regulated customers needed data residency and audit logs before they needed features. Getting compliant took a year and opened up healthcare, education and public-sector work.

  5. 2026

    47ms median, 210+ locations

    A quarter spent instrumenting the whole delivery path cut median glass-to-glass latency by 78%. We wrote up every change on the blog, including the ones that did not work.

How we work

Four things we actually do

Not aspirations on a wall — these are the rules we argue about in code review.

Publish the number

Latency, uptime and rebuffer ratio go on the status page whether they flatter us or not. A benchmark you cannot reproduce is marketing, and we have all been lied to by enough of it.

You cannot roll back a broadcast

Live has no second take, so we deploy behind flags, drain regions slowly and rehearse failure. Anyone can stop a release; nobody has to justify having stopped one.

Engineers answer support

Every engineer takes a support shift. It is the fastest way to learn which parts of the API are confusing, and it makes the roadmap argument short.

Write the post-mortem in public

Outages get a public write-up with a timeline, a root cause and the guardrail we added. No blame, no passive voice, no “an issue was experienced”.
